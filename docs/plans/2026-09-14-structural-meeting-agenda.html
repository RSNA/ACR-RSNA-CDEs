Structural discussion agenda

14 September meeting agenda · Revised 15 September 2026

Build on the alpha implementation. Start with anatomy, work through incompatible meanings, then consider useful extensions and unresolved questions.

Purpose of the meeting

Identify where our ideas fit the foundation, where we need to reconcile approaches, and which examples would help us decide. The positions below are proposals for discussion, not a settled model that the alpha must conform to.

Clinical-content cleanup and mat-based presentation work remain separate. Versioning is deferred. The diagrams here illustrate discussion points; they do not change the implemented schema.

1

The anatomy axis

Establish the shared substrate, develop tissue-type and structure-type scope specifiers, and connect them to the anatomic location hierarchy.

Start from the overlay, together with RadLex

The working direction is the anatomic-locations data as an overlay on RadLex and the basis of the evolving anatomy axis—not a replacement ontology or the sole source. Reference the pinned data rather than reproduce it. A location’s identity is its RadLex ID; compound sided IDs are temporary exceptions to resolve through the anatomy track.

Develop two families of anatomy scope specifiers

Tissue typesPulmonary parenchyma, hepatic parenchyma, subcutaneous fat.

Structure typesSolid organs; vessels with artery/vein subtypes; muscles, tendons, ligaments.

The open structural task: figure out how these scope specifiers connect to the anatomic location hierarchy. These are initial families to develop, not a decision to introduce separate graph node types or make the families mutually exclusive.

Exploratory connection ideas—not adopted: compare explicit tissue/location nodes with tissue scope specifiers linked to locations. Explore is-a classification for structure kinds, with separately identified part-of/location links for tissue setting. Keep an organ distinct from its parenchyma. A named artery, hepatic parenchyma, and subcutaneous fat in a region could make useful comparison cases; no connection model has been selected.

  1. Bring the refactor into view. Which revision should we examine next, and how does it connect to the overlay? Retire questions already answered by that work; the earlier snapshot is not evidence of the refactor’s behavior.
  2. Fill the principal gap: is-a and structure types. The pinned dataset has locations and spatial relationships but lacks an is-a relation and the structure-type nodes needed for “applies to tendons” or “muscle, NOS.” Discuss a first layer derived from RadLex, reviewed for questionable source classifications and handed back to the anatomy track.
  3. Keep the relationships distinct. Agree names and meanings for is-a, part-of, spatial containment, and sided/unsided links. For each scope check, identify allowed paths and direction. A part is not automatically a subtype of its whole; containment is not automatically partonomy.
  4. Exercise anatomy use directly. Walk through a sided location satisfying an unsided scope, tissue-type and structure-type scopes, and a normal-anatomy measurement. Our preference is to bind DataElements and Measurements directly to AnatomicLocations, without manufacturing a finding. The Observation already specifies a sided location; the new work concerns how the scope specifiers connect to that hierarchy, not a redesign of Observation laterality.
  5. Keep two actionable lists. Maintain the gap log separately from specific node requests. Current requests include lung parenchyma, perirenal space and sided forms, pericardial space, subarachnoid space, and permanent IDs for compound sided variants. Pleural space is already present and should not remain an “absent node” request.

Leave this section with: the revision to review, a starting set of tissue/structure scope specifiers, examples to compare their hierarchy connections, and a route for the type layer and verified node requests. Newly named candidates are not automatically confirmed missing nodes.

2

Apparent active conflicts

Places where two expressed approaches appear to give different answers—not merely features an alpha has not implemented yet.

Evidence boundary: implementation observations below come from the previously reviewed snapshot ae08188534fd. Confirm whether each still applies to the refactor before treating it as a current conflict. Our alternative positions remain negotiable.

2.1 Does taxonomy supply definition bindings?

Reviewed alphaCompiled subtype views acquire ancestor elements, measurements, and other relationships.

Our initial proposalClassification and directly authored definition bindings are separate; an is-a edge does not automatically supply those bindings.

What is inheritance intended to accomplish? Which consequences do we want, and which should require explicit authorship?

2.2 Finding and Diagnosis: types, roles, or disjoint classes?

Reviewed alphaFindingClass and Diagnosis are disjoint in OWL, and generated taxonomy is limited to findings.

Our proposalA clinically useful taxonomy can cross finding/diagnosis labels, while retaining useful distinctions between definitions.

What do the labels classify? Can we preserve the useful type distinctions without excluding cross-label taxonomy or associations?

2.3 A categorizing element or named subtypes?

Reviewed alphaSome named classes are defined through necessary-and-sufficient descriptor conditions.

Our position to discussFor the same categorization in the same model, choose either an element’s values or named subtypes. In the taxonomy version, omit that categorizing element.

Where are both currently representing the same distinction? Can different contexts choose different representations without requiring duplicate categorization within one definition?

2.4 An assessment scheme is not one category list

Reviewed alphaAssessmentScheme has embedded categories; scheme and component structure are not separated as we envisage.

Our proposalKeep AssessmentScheme as a separate type, linked to multiple ordinary DataElements, each with its own values.

Can the existing scheme type accommodate multiple dimensions, as in Lung-RADS or a staging system, without conflating the scheme with one component or its selected value?

2.5 Do per-use restrictions keep their meaning across exports?

Reviewed alphaJSON filters a binding’s allowed values; OWL records narrowing as an annotation. Per-use modality is annotated on a shared property.

Our position to discussA value subset or modality limit belongs to that use. It should remain identifiable and should not silently change every use of the shared descriptor.

Can each consumer recover the same binding-local restriction? An annotation may be appropriate, but what must the consuming application do with it?

For each apparent conflict: confirm current behavior, explain the intended benefit, record agreement or disagreement, and choose one example that distinguishes the alternatives.

3

Ideas for improving the foundation

Potential additions or modifications. These are not claims that the current foundation is wrong.

Component counts and component-of scope

Explore one component relationship carrying minimum/maximum counts, plus an optional component-of scope: the family of findings a component can belong inside. In this proposal, a scoped component cannot stand alone; an unscoped class can. Keep the allowed container family distinct from the actual container in a report.

Does this fit the alpha’s component structure? Test optional, exactly-one, and multiple independently describable components without turning their counts into required report fields.

Reusable descriptors with precise local use

  • Value subsets: reuse one DataElement while allowing a binding to select only some of its values. Discuss the cross-export issue above.
  • Selection cardinality: propose a default on DataElement, with possible adjustment on a binding. Whether an adjustment can broaden the default remains open.
  • Ordering and methods: retain distinct DataElement and Measurement types; distinguish ordered from unordered value sets. Our preference is that measurement method is optional and unspecified by default.
  • Three levels of modality: distinguish finding visibility, descriptor applicability in one finding’s binding, and intrinsic descriptor capability. Our position is that a missing constraint means no information, not “all modalities”; an intrinsic limit cannot be overridden by a use.
  • Multi-component measurements: explore the idea, without advancing it as an adopted model or a rejection of derivation links. Clarify grouping versus calculation.

Incremental export improvements · later implementation

Keep these suggestions as a downstream implementation backlog, not additional structural questions that must be resolved in this meeting.

  1. Preserve authored relationship IDs and properties in a full representation; let compact views refer back to them.
  2. Make referenced diagnoses, descriptors, schemes, and anatomy resolvable without forcing every type into a finding-shaped export.
  3. Document each export’s purpose, intentional omissions, and treatment of missing information.
  4. Use a few shared examples to compare JSON and OWL answers, adding proposed fields only when their meanings are agreed.
  5. Expose enough relationship detail for a later mat-based view. Plan the presentation adaptation separately from structural decisions.

Leave this section with: which structural proposals are useful, which need an example, and which to defer. Export implementation can follow once the relevant meanings are agreed.

4

Open work and deferred implementation

The original seven broad questions mixed existing working-model answers with downstream implementation details. They are withdrawn as framed.

Carry forward the actual open anatomy work

Develop the tissue-type and structure-type scope families and work out their connections to the anatomic location hierarchy. Compare the exploratory alternatives from section 1 without assuming one has been adopted. Carry unresolved specific conflicts and proposals forward from sections 2 and 3; do not manufacture another general question list.

Existing working-model answers

  • Measurements: represented by Measurement nodes. This does not change the separate proposal for AssessmentScheme with ordinary DataElements.
  • Side: the Observation specifies a sided anatomic location.
  • Absence: an Observation can point to a FindingClass with a component value referencing the presence DataElement and value “absent.”
  • Scope combinations: our working position is any-match (OR) for a plain list, with explicit all-match (AND) combinations also supported. The remaining encoding work is not a new choice of intended meaning.

Keep these in the implementation backlog

Check that OWL mappings preserve the definition/Observation distinction; refine export metadata and terminology mappings; and develop verification checks. The earlier reference to “incomplete anatomy” meant missing taxonomy coverage, which belongs in the anatomy gap work. None of these broad questions establishes an additional structural conflict.

Close with a short decision record

For each item reached, record: agreed / still open / deferred, the intended meaning, a distinguishing example, and who takes the next action. Preserve the reasons for unresolved differences.

Separate follow-up: clinical-content corrections and adaptation of the presentation toward mat views. Deferred: versioning.
