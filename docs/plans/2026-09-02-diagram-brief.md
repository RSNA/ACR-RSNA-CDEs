**Written:** 2026-09-02. **Status:** superseded. This was the round-one brief; its ranked list was overtaken by the owner's decisions the same day (item 5, anatomic scope where it differs, was struck). ## Why these diagrams exist

The vocabulary is a graph: finding classes, diagnoses, grouping nodes, data elements, anatomy, and typed relationships between them. The committee that has to approve it is made of radiologists, not ontologists. A "constellation" diagram is the picture that lets one of them look at a real clinical family (acute pyelonephritis; pleural effusion) and, in under a minute, either agree that the model says what they believe or point at the thing that is wrong. The diagram is an argument, not decoration. If a radiologist cannot argue with it, it has failed.

## What must be visible at a glance

Ranked. A design that nails 1 to 3 and treats 4 and 5 as secondary is acceptable; the reverse is not.

1. **The taxonomy, including where it crosses the finding/diagnosis label.** Empyema is a kind of pleural effusion even though one is a diagnosis and the other a finding. Renal abscess is a kind of renal lesion. Above both sits a grouping node (`renal abnormality`, `pleural abnormality`) that only ever appears negated in a report. Subsumption must read as containment or descent, not as one more arrow among many.
2. **The typed relationships between classes, with their properties unambiguously attached.** Manifestation edges (`MAY_MANIFEST_AS`) carry typicality (obligate, very frequent, frequent, occasional, very rare, excluded) and specificity (pathognomonic, highly suggestive, suggestive, or none). Causal edges (`MAY_CAUSE`) carry typicality and an `expected` hint about the caused entity (heart failure: bilateral, right greater than left, simple fluid; cirrhosis: right-sided; pancreatitis: left-sided; malignancy: large, recurs). Progression (`MAY_PROGRESS_TO`) and co-occurrence (`OCCURS_WITH`) exist and must look different from causation and manifestation. When five causes converge on one class, the reader must still see which typicality belongs to which cause.
3. **Direction and kind of node.** Diagnosis, finding, grouping, anatomy, element are different kinds of thing and must be distinguishable without a legend after the first look.
4. **What a class binds**: its data elements (presence, size, distribution, fluid attenuation, internal complexity), and the special case where a class is an interpretation of a measurement bound to anatomy (renal enlargement `INTERPRETED_FROM` the kidney's length binding).
5. **Anatomic scope**, where it differs within a family (perinephric fat stranding is scoped to the perirenal space, everything else to the kidney).

## Hard constraints

- **Generated, not drawn.** The final diagram must be producible deterministically by a script from the canonical graph files plus at most a small "view" file naming which nodes to include. Hand-authored SVG is fine for a *mockup*, but the design must have a credible deterministic layout algorithm, and the README must say what it is.
- **Static SVG.** Inline in an HTML page (nodes can be links) and as an image in Markdown on GitHub. No JavaScript layout, no interactivity required for comprehension.
- **Fits a document.** About 1000 to 1300 px wide; height is free within reason. Print-legible in grayscale is a plus.
- **Scales** from 10 to about 30 nodes.
- **Both examples from one design.** Pyelonephritis (two-family tree plus manifestations) and pleural effusion (fan-in of causes, subtypes under a finding, companions) must both work.

## Directions worth exploring (pick two that are genuinely different)

- Nested containment: subtypes drawn inside their parent's box, so subsumption is literally containment; relationships drawn between boxes.
- A manifestation matrix: diagnoses as rows, findings as columns, typicality and specificity as glyphs in the cells; causes and companions as flanking lists.
- Hub-and-spoke centered on the finding or diagnosis under discussion, spokes grouped by relationship type in sectors.
- Swimlanes by node kind with orthogonal (elbow) routing and labels placed on the horizontal run of each edge.
- Reading order: laid out the way a radiologist reads a case, findings first, then the diagnosis, then complications and causes.
- Small multiples: one compact card per diagnosis listing its manifestations as rows with typicality and specificity pills, cards arranged to show subtype descent.
- Something else entirely, if you can defend it against the ranked list above.
